## The headline

**$11,840 of your $47,310 went to people searching for Northbound Outdoors by name.** That is 25% of the account, and almost all of it is inside one Performance Max campaign that reports a 9.4x return.

---

## Brand leak

One in four dollars bought a click from someone who already knew who you were and typed your name into Google.

| Campaign | Total spend | Brand spend | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| PMax - All Products | $28,900 | $9,220 | 32% |
| Shopping - Core | $12,410 | $2,180 | 18% |
| Search - Generic | $6,000 | $440 | 7% |

Performance Max bids on your brand by default and records every one of those sales as a win. That is why it is your best-performing campaign on paper.

The PMax figure is a floor rather than a total, because Performance Max reports search categories instead of actual search terms, so some brand traffic is sitting inside broader labels where it cannot be counted.

**About $18,400 of the revenue credited to these campaigns looks like sales you were getting anyway.** That applies an industry median to your numbers rather than measuring your store. Stella's analysis of 225 geo-holdout tests puts the median incremental return on branded search at 0.70x, the lowest of any channel. The only way to know your own figure is to run a holdout test.

Brand spend is not automatically waste. Some stores defend their name on purpose because competitors bid on it. The finding here is that it is a default rather than a decision.

## Profit per campaign

Your blended gross margin is 41%, from cost per item on 94% of line items. Six percent of revenue had no SKU match, mostly bundles.

That puts your **break-even ROAS at 2.44x**. Below that, a campaign costs money to run no matter what the ROAS column says.

Two things worth your attention.

**Shopping - Clearance looks like your second-best campaign and is not.** Its products run at 22% margin, so it needs 4.55x just to stand still. At 4.60x it is running to a standstill. An account-wide ROAS target would tell you this campaign is winning.

**Search - Generic is below break-even.** It is small, so the loss is small, but it has been losing money for the whole window.

## Reconciliation

| | Google Ads | Shopify | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conversions / orders | 1,412 | 1,377 | +2.5% |
| Value / net revenue | $284,600 | $251,200 | +13.3% |

The order counts agree, which is a good sign and rarer than it should be.

The value does not, and 13.3% is close enough to GST to be worth checking. If your conversion value includes tax, every ROAS in this report is inflated by the same amount and so is your break-even target.

Open Google Ads → Goals → Conversions → Purchase and check what the value is built from. Twenty minutes, and it changes every other number here.

## Three fixes this week

**1. Check what your conversion value includes.** Twenty minutes.

Google Ads → Goals → Conversions → Purchase. If the value includes GST or delivery, every target in the account is set against an inflated number. Confirm before you act on anything else in this report, because everything here is calculated from that figure.

**2. Split brand out of Performance Max.** Half a day.

Add your brand as a negative in PMax and run brand traffic through a campaign you control. At $11,840 over 90 days, you decide how much of that you want to keep paying for rather than having it decided for you. Some of it is worth defending. Not all of it.

Expect your reported ROAS to fall when you do this. The revenue does not go anywhere, only the credit for it.

**3. Rewrite your top ten product titles.** Two hours.

Brand, then product type, then the attributes people search. Keep what matters in the first 70 characters. Pull the real queries from your search terms report for each product and use their words rather than yours.
