# Negative keyword seed list

Check these against the store's own search terms report. A seed goes on the printed list only when it cost money in this account and passed the checks below. Never output this list on its own, because a generic list is worth very little and the owner can find one anywhere. The value is in the terms that came out of their account. Seeds that never appeared in the account are a precaution. Offer them on request, and do not print them as if they were a finding.

**Always show what each block will block before recommending it.** Some of these are wrong for some stores, and the notes say which.

**Check every seed against the account before it goes on the list.** Work out the pattern figures in `method.md` § Wasted spend first. A seed whose pattern has converted in this account stays off the list, and the report names it. Retailer names are the usual surprise, because someone comparing shops can still buy from you. The same goes for a seed that is a product word in this store's catalogue, and for the other spellings of a seed that converted. `method.md` § The seed list has the full rules.

Store brief question 15 says whether these blocks are safe for this store: Marketplaces, Second-hand and free (with the second-hand lists in the country blocks), Buy now pay later, Wrong buyer, and Repair and parts. With no answer, leave them off.

## Country defaults

Pick the blocks for the markets the store sells into. A store sells into a market when at least a tenth of its orders ship there, a campaign is named for it, or the owner says it advertises there. Never apply a block for a market it does not sell into. A store selling somewhere with no block here uses the other sections only. A generic negative list will not have these names, and they are often where the money goes.

⚠️ **Some retailers are named after ordinary words.** Next, Boots, Very and The Range in the UK. Target, Ross, Gap, Chewy and Goodwill in the US, and Target in Australia too. As a single-word negative, "next" blocks "next day delivery" and "boots" blocks "walking boots". Leave these names out unless the search terms report shows the retailer meaning, and then add only the exact phrase you saw, such as "target store" or "chewy.com".

**Buy now pay later** terms sit in every country block. People searching these are shopping for a payment method, not a product, and they convert badly against a product ad. **Do not apply** them if the store's main selling point is that it offers these, or if it sells a high-ticket item where payment terms drive the decision. Ask first.

## How to apply

Cover these in the report in two or three sentences, the way the example report does.

1. **Start at campaign level, not account level.** An account-level negative applies to everything including campaigns you have not looked at yet.
2. **Single words go in as broad-match negatives. Multi-word patterns go in as phrase-match negatives**, in quotes. Exact-match negatives only block that exact string and almost never do what people expect.
3. **Add them to a shared negative keyword list** rather than pasting into each campaign, so there is one place to manage them.
4. **Re-pull the search terms report in 30 days.** New terms arrive constantly, and the list is a habit rather than a one-off job.
5. **Do not negative anything that has converted**, even once, even if it looks wrong. Check before you paste.

The fastest way to make an account worse is to bulk-paste a negative list without checking it against conversions.
